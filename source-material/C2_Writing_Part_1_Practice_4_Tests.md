## Practice Test 1 — Sport in Schools

### Part 1

Read the two texts below.

Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas on the topic.

Write your answer in **240–280 words**.

### Text 1

#### Raising the Bar of School Sports

The National Sports Academy is running a series of workshops aimed at encouraging more teenagers to get involved in sport. The workshops are for teachers and will look at how to promote the numerous benefits of physical exercise and team sports to school pupils.

“Exercise helps to maintain a stable weight, strengthen bones and reduce the risk of stress-related illnesses,” explained Tim Collins, the Academy Director. “Aside from the health benefits, taking part in competitive sports gives teenagers a chance to experience the highs and lows of competition, as well as instilling discipline, responsibility and commitment — all valuable life skills.”

### Text 2

#### Sport Seen as Uncool

Teenagers are spending less time on physical exercise because they find it deeply “uncool”, according to a survey of teachers and pupils. The problem is most prevalent among older teenagers.

Girls are uncomfortable about sharing sports classes with boys and avoid swimming lessons because of a fear that it will spoil their hairstyles. Both sexes were also scathing in the survey about the unfashionable PE uniform they had to wear. The choice of sports available was considered too limited as well.

Teachers said they would like to see dress codes for sports kits relaxed, single-sex physical education classes introduced, and a wider range of activities offered to combat the decline in physical activity.

## Practice Test 2 — Modern Eating Habits

### Part 1

Read the two texts below.

Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas on the topic.

Write your answer in **240–280 words**.

### Text 1

#### Our Relationship with Food

The British relationship with food has undergone a fundamental change over the past 50 years, and in quite a contradictory fashion. Meals were once made up of simple, local ingredients using recipes that had been followed by previous generations.

Now food has become a sensory and cultural phenomenon. Food production and catering form a multi-million-pound industry. Top chefs show us how to cook the most delicious three-course meals, and supermarkets provide all the ingredients we need.

Yet, at the same time, we are less willing to spend time cooking than was once the case and instead spend huge amounts on takeaways and ready meals.

### Text 2

#### The Ready-Meal Industry

The latest figures show that the UK ready-meal industry is experiencing continued growth despite the recent economic downturn. The reason for this success is that time-poor consumers continue to want exotic, convenient mealtime solutions while also being cautious about overspending on takeaways and trips to restaurants.

The future looks even brighter for this industry, with innovative and niche sectors opening up to cater for as wide a range of tastes as possible.

As for the meals people are eating, Italian remains the favourite cuisine, closely followed by Indian and Chinese food.

## Practice Test 3 — Wealth and Economic Inequality

### Part 1

Read the two texts below.

Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas on the topic.

Write your answer in **240–280 words**.

### Text 1

#### The Vital Role of High-Net-Worth Individuals

Wealthy individuals make an invaluable contribution to modern economies through their entrepreneurial activities, investment and philanthropy. The top 1% of earners typically contribute over a third of all income-tax revenue, effectively subsidising public services for the wider population.

Their business ventures create thousands of jobs, while their consumer spending supports countless luxury and service industries. Many wealthy individuals establish charitable foundations that address critical social issues more efficiently than government programmes.

Their investment in start-ups and innovative technologies also drives economic growth and technological advancement. Furthermore, their international business activities attract foreign investment and enhance global trade relationships, benefiting the broader economy through increased economic opportunities and knowledge transfer.

### Text 2

#### A System Rigged in Favour of the Wealthy

Despite claims about their economic contribution, the ultra-wealthy consistently avoid paying their fair share through sophisticated tax-avoidance schemes and offshore arrangements. While their nominal tax contributions may seem large, they often pay a lower effective tax rate than middle-income workers.

The supposed job-creation benefits of their businesses frequently come with poor working conditions and suppressed wages. Their philanthropic activities, while widely praised, often serve as tax write-offs and provide them with undue influence over social policy.

The concentration of wealth has reached unprecedented levels, with the richest 1% now owning more wealth than half the world’s population combined. This accumulation perpetuates inequality through privileged access to education, healthcare and investment opportunities, effectively pulling up the ladder behind them.

## Practice Test 4 — Censorship and Freedom of Speech

### Part 1

Read the two texts below.

Write an essay summarising and evaluating the key points from both texts. Use your own words throughout as far as possible, and include your own ideas on the topic.

Write your answer in **240–280 words**.

### Text 1

#### Censorship — A Necessary Evil

We are constantly being exposed to dangerous opinions and need to be protected from them, just as we need to protect children from bad influences on television. This is even more important in the age of the internet, when anyone can write whatever they want and upload it to a webpage.

The voices of those who speak out against the establishment should also be silenced, as they aim to undermine the stability of the state. Free speech should only be granted to those prepared to use it responsibly.

### Text 2

#### Freedom of Speech Is a Human Right

Free speech uses the word “free” for a reason, and any limitation on it amounts to censorship. Censorship infringes civil and human rights and cannot be allowed in any form in a democratic country.

Many governments use censorship as a way of silencing their critics, thus robbing ordinary citizens of the right to be informed. Censorship begins with a few words being removed here and there and ends with newspapers being closed down and voices of protest being imprisoned.

Under no circumstances, therefore, can censorship be condoned.
